The terms Access Point and Wireless Access Point may be used interchangeably. 

Wireless Access Points allow devices to wirelessly connect to a wired network. It acts as a bridge which converts wireless signals to wired ones and vice versa.

They can be used to reduce the need for hardwire connections or extend signal range and strength of wireless networks. 

**Common AP Configs**
**Root Access Point** - AP is connected to a wired LAN, allowing for wireless connections
**Repeater Access Point** - AP or extenders can extend the range of your wireless infrastructure. This can pass traffic through other repeaters until it reaches the AP connected to the wired network.
**Bridges** - APs can join multiple networks
**Workgroup Bridge** - Can allow traditionally wired devices to be connected to a WLAN by bridging them to the wireless LAN via an AP.
**Central unit in all-wireless network** - An AP can function as a hub for all the devices on an already predominantly wireless network. This increases the communication range.
